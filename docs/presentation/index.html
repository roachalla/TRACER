What I tried

First, I tried audio enhancement with:

  • Descript Studio Sound
  • Adobe Audition
  • Resemble Enhance
  • Adobe Podcast Enhance (now Audio Enhance in Adobe Firefly)
Outcome

Adobe Podcast Enhance improved sound quality noticeably; the others were not able to remediate it. But captions remained problematic.

Problem

Challenging transcripts

Room noise, microphone quality, the instructor's accent and complex content all contributed to very low accuracy captions.

What I tried
  1. Comparing different online transcription tools

    YuJa, Clipchamp, YouTube

  2. Comparing local speech engines

    Whisper, Parakeet, Qwen

  3. Creating a script to combine transcripts to increase accuracy
  • None of the speech engines could independently reach a workable level of accuracy.
  • Adding an LLM loop to compare and resolve gaps improved accuracy a great deal, but introduced inaccuracies and hallucinations.

Problem

Complex slides challenge OCR

Each slide layers typed text, black handwritten derivations and red annotations: circles, arrows, strike-throughs.

OCR alone struggled with red ink and with telling the layers apart. Scanning each frame with vision was prohibitively expensive.

Approach
  1. Programmatically select frames

    Keep the last frame before each change. The video is sampled every 20 seconds; a frame is kept when the next one differs, so each finished state is captured once.

    Deterministic · ffmpeg + OpenCV
  2. Scan symbols and numbers using OCR

    Mathpix is about 40% cheaper than Claude vision.

    Mathpix API
  3. Use LLM vision in a comprehension pass

    Boost the OCR accuracy. The vision reader's equations are published; Mathpix is the character-level cross-check.

    LLM vision · Claude Sonnet
  4. Cross-check the two readings

    About 70% agree and are used as they are (112 of 159 equations across three lectures). The remaining 30% are re-read against the slide, other video frames and the instructor's words; only what can't be settled goes to designer and SME review, with both readings shown.

    Deterministic · Python
  5. Repair questioned equations

    Re-read against the slide; a second model confirms. The repair loop keeps the instructor's own slips; it only fixes misreadings.

    LLM · Claude Sonnet + Haiku

Problem

Which marks matter?

Drawings raised several challenges:

  1. Drawings must be identified and extracted from the video. Automated passes may skip some figures and include others more than once.
  2. Each graphic must be placed at the right point in the transcript.
  3. Items that should be transcribed were sometimes captured as images, most often equations, so the pipeline screens for them.
  4. Unguided LLM descriptions do not meet my criteria for STEM long descriptions, so the model needed specific guidance.
Approach
  1. Find and crop figures

    Repeated crops are removed automatically.

    Mathpix API + deterministic
  2. Describe in context

    The crop, the slide text and the instructor's words.

    LLM vision · Claude Sonnet
  3. Apply our description guide

    Decorative, alt text only, or a layered long description.

    In-house long-description guide
  4. Turn drawn equations into math

    Equations drawn as images become real math.

    LLM vision · Claude Sonnet
  5. Identify issues that can't be resolved with a high level of confidence and list them for manual reviewReview page

Caption

Figure 1. Definition sketch of surface waves of wavelength λ on a pool of finite depth H above a flat bed.

Long description
  • Hand-drawn definition sketch: a wavy water surface at the top, a flat hatched bottom, and a vertical depth H between them.
  • Top: a wavy line is the free surface. A short double-headed horizontal arrow spanning one wave, labelled λ, marks the wavelength.
  • Bottom: a slightly sloping straight line with short hatch marks below it represents the solid bed.
  • Middle: a long double-headed vertical arrow runs from the surface down to the bed, labelled H, marking the water depth.
  • The wavelength is drawn very small compared with the depth H in the sketch.
  • A partly cut-off handwritten mark at the bottom right edge is unreadable.

What I tried

I also attempted delegating transcript reconstruction and modality conversion in its entirety to an agentic workflow to see how well it handled this challenge without my direction. I initially attempted reconstruction and conversion in Claude Desktop, Gemini Desktop and ChatGPT Desktop.

Outcome
  • Gemini completely failed, but Claude and ChatGPT Desktop easily recreated and converted video content to HTML or Word document formats, regenerating poor-quality graphics, creating long descriptions and encoding math accurately and accessibly.
  • However, they were unable to accurately transcribe audio or combine visuals with the transcript in a logical and coherent order.
  • Also, this process was expensive and a bit unpredictable. One run of a 12-minute video in Claude Code used 5% of my weekly Pro quota. Both Claude and ChatGPT took liberties to install and run things on the GPU without consulting me, sometimes installing things that were already installed.
  • Reconstruction attempts were fabricating anywhere from 60 to 100% of the video narration, based on the video images. This approach may work for lower-level courses, but for highly specialized and complex content, these products were not viable due to loss of content integrity.

TipUse Plan mode, so you are aware of and agree with how the agent plans to accomplish the task.

Problem

Reconstruction pulls toward the generic

I work with STEM professors who are renowned experts in their field. Each of these instructors has a unique teaching “fingerprint”: a combination of their experience and insight, the terminology they use to describe their field, and the method or approach they take to certain types of problems. Their language carries their expertise.

LLMs predict likely continuations based on their training data, which may include out-of-date textbooks or papers from other experts. When reconstructing or converting transcripts, if not given strict guardrails, the LLM will introduce terminology and sequencing that is not representative of the instructor's “fingerprint”.

My process introduces multiple levels of audits, reviews and blind readers, to ensure the product is as close to the instructor's original language and reasoning as the evidence allows.

Approach
  1. Write only from evidence

    Every sentence cites its transcript or slide source.

    LLM · Claude Sonnet
  2. Check every sentence

    Numbers and quotes must match the cited source.

    Deterministic · Python
  3. Audit for additions

    A separate model flags anything the instructor did not say.

    LLM · Claude Haiku
  4. Peer review and repair

    An expert-level read; questioned equations are re-checked.

    LLM · Claude Sonnet + Haiku
  5. Expert sign-off

    A short, evidence-backed list instead of a re-watch.

    Instructor + designer

TRACER brings each of these solutions together into one automated pipeline, so the videos for an entire course can be batch-processed.
